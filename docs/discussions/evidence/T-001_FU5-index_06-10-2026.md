## 3. Claude's reading (analysis, NOT evidence; for QA to audit)
- **Provenance.** Every one of the 64 routines belongs to exactly one extension. cron 7 = `pg_cron` 1.6.4; net 5 = `pg_net` 0.19.5; vault 3 = `supabase_vault` 0.3.1; schema `extensions` 49 = `pgcrypto` 1.3 (36) + `uuid-ossp` 1.1 (10) + `pg_stat_statements` 1.11 (3). Extension owners: `supabase_admin` (pg_cron, pg_net, supabase_vault), `postgres` (the other three). No routine has an owner different from its extension's owner and none has a library name that differs from its extension name (zero `owner_differs_from_extension_owner`, `library_name_differs_from_extension`, `owner_not_postgres_or_supabase_admin`).
- **Declared properties (a statement of what the catalog shows, not a safety conclusion; QA Round 62, non-blocking 2).** None is SECURITY DEFINER, none has function-level configuration, all are plain functions (`kind` f). One returns a trigger: `cron.job_cache_invalidate()` (pg_cron's own job-cache trigger function, executable by PUBLIC).
- **Library.** 59 routines use `$libdir/<extension>`; the 5 `pg_net` routines (`net._encode_url_with_params_array`, `net._urlencode_string`, `net.wait_until_running`, `net.wake`, `net.worker_restart`) record the bare library name `pg_net` and a default ACL: the only `library_not_in_libdir` leads. This is a declared-property difference, not a finding of harm.
- **ACLs.** 59 have an explicit ACL, 5 (the same pg_net routines) have the default ACL. Executable by `anon` and `authenticated`: 58; by `service_role`: 59. The six routines **not** executable by `anon` or `authenticated` are `cron.alter_job`, `cron.schedule_in_database`, `extensions.pg_stat_statements_reset` and the three `vault._crypto_aead_det_*` helpers (of these only `_crypto_aead_det_decrypt` is executable, by `service_role`).
- **Question raised, NOT answered by this evidence.** `cron.schedule(...)` (two forms) and `cron.unschedule(...)` (two forms) are executable by PUBLIC, hence by `anon` and `authenticated`, according to `has_function_privilege`, which tests EXECUTE on the function only and not USAGE on the schema. Whether a client role can actually reach the `cron` schema, and what scheduling a job would let it do, is **not measured** (a schema-USAGE and cron-job-ownership check would measure it). This matters to the T-001 boundary, because a scheduled job runs SQL and the guard design must not be bypassable by one; J5 showed only two active jobs and no group-table mention. Claude proposes to carry this as a lead into plan v6, not to assert a gap.
- **What stays blocking.** Per plan v5 section 3 and QA Round 58, every one of the 64 routines remains a positive lead until its captured identity, version, library symbol and ACL are classified in a reviewed baseline or manifest; extension membership is provenance, not proof of harmlessness; C behaviour is not inferable from catalogs.
